Basic Gaussians

C++ · raylib · WebAssembly Project 16

The splat, on its own, before any of the training. A 2D gaussian is a centre, a 2×2 covariance and a colour, and rendering one is a single exponential per pixel. Place a few and they add together into something that looks nothing like the blurry blobs it is made of. Article coming soon.


Live demo

Click empty space to drop a splat, click one to select it, drag to move. The sliders retune whatever is selected, and whatever they are left on is what the next click places.

  • σx, σy the two axis lengths of the covariance, in pixels
  • rotation spins those axes, which is the off-diagonal term
  • weight peak amplitude, before anything overlaps
  • colour what the splat contributes where it lands
  • exposure tone mapping only, applied after the splats are summed
  • outlines the one-sigma ellipse of every splat, and the hit region for a click

What is actually running

Each splat is evaluated as exp(-0.5 (p - mu)^T sigma^-1 (p - mu)) into a float buffer that the splats add into, and the sum is Reinhard tone mapped down to 8 bit for display. That is why overlaps go pale rather than clipping to white. The sliders never touch sigma directly: three free numbers in a symmetric matrix is not something anyone can aim, so the controls edit two axis lengths and an angle, and the C++ composes sigma = R S R^T from them. The status line under the controls prints the matrix that comes out.

Splatting only runs over a four standard deviation box around the centre. Past that the exponential is under a thousandth of the peak, and covering the full 800×800 for every splat is the difference between a drag that tracks the cursor and one that does not.
