Exploring NEAT and RL with a simple 2D Counter-Strike game

11 min read
By Declan Porter

I've been deep diving Reinforcement Learning (RL) these last few months. Read most of Intro to RL by stutton ( put link ) You can find these implementation here ( put link to arcade folder in github) I've gone from basic tabular to full PPO. That's been great, and is where SOA of the industry mind power is. But I've always found the NEAT algorithm facinating. Ever since I saw it playing Mario as a kid ( put link to vid ), I wanted to understand it and get it to play my games. Back then however I was too busy making games of my own, that's how I landed up in AAA. I did attempt to implement it once but it's a tricky algorimth, at least at first. Now however, with more years to my name, and with handle AI models that will sit there a explain and critque without hesitation I decided to take another go at it. The goal is to make a bot that plays a litte top down shooter game, then create keep inceasing the complexity. Below is some of the journey and the issues I'm having.

Part-1 the enviroment and features

Show screenshot

Basic rules, empty map. Shoot the other player until you win. Very basic. I started training it, but platued almost imdetially. Training against the programmed bot way to difficult to get a reasonable reward through So I moved to self-play quickly. Using the programmed bot as an evaulation metric. The idea is quiet simple, have a tournament with a champoin that plays against the programmed bot. Each agent will have an ELO which is was scores them against each other. After 50 generations we get something like this

Both bots decide to run to the corner and continue firing randomly until one of them dies. Not exactly intellegent behavior. The network at this point has about 1 hidden layer, 13 hidden nodes. Unsure what to do at his point, I decided to let it run for another 100 generations
